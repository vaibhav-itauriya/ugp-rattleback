      What is being simulated

      A semi-ellipsoid with semi-axes a₁, a₂, a₃ whose principal axes of inertia are turned slightly against its axes of curvature (the product of inertia I₁₂). Spun one way it keeps spinning; spun the other way it starts to rock and comes out spinning backwards.

      With rolling the contact point never slips, nothing is lost, and the contact force is recovered after the fact. The other models let the contact point slip and solve nine equations: the angular velocity, the 1–2–3 Euler angles, and the velocity of the centre of mass.

      The normal reaction is solved, not assumed

      Requiring the centre of mass to stay at the height set by the contact geometry gives the normal reaction in closed form:

      N = m (g + c + d·A₀) / (1 − m d·B − m d·A₁)

      For these parameters it swings between 0.20 mg and 5.86 mg during a rolling reversal, so taking N = mg is not safe.

      Contact laws

      • Coulomb: F = −μN vP/√(|vP|² + ε²), after Moffatt and Shimomura (2002).
      • Drilling: an extra torque about the vertical of size up to μNρ, from friction over a small contact patch.
      • Viscous: F = −c vP, the second law of Moffatt and Shimomura.

      How it runs

      An adaptive Dormand–Prince integrator runs in your browser, in a background thread. The Coulomb law is stiff, so high friction or a small ε means smaller steps: the default case takes about a second, μ = 1 several seconds.

      Every run reports four checks: the contact residual, whether the energy only goes down, whether N stays positive (the body never leaves the table), and whether the denominator of the N formula stays positive.
