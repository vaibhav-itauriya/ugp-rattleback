    What is simulated

    A semi-ellipsoid with semi-axes a₁, a₂, a₃ whose principal axes of inertia are turned slightly against its axes of curvature, through the product of inertia I₁₂. Spun one way it keeps spinning; spun the other way it rocks and comes out spinning backwards.

    Rolling forbids slip: six equations, no energy loss, and the contact force is recovered after the fact. Coulomb and viscous let the contact point slip: nine equations for the angular velocity, the 1–2–3 Euler angles and the velocity of the centre of mass.

    The normal reaction is solved, not assumed

    Keeping the centre of mass at the height set by the contact geometry gives

    N = m (g + c + d·A₀) / (1 − m d·B − m d·A₁)

    During a rolling reversal it swings between 0.20 mg and 5.86 mg, so N = mg is not a safe shortcut.

    Contact laws

    • Coulomb: F = −μN vP/√(|vP|² + ε²), after Moffatt and Shimomura (2002).
    • Drilling: a torque about the vertical of size up to μNρ, from friction over a small contact patch.
    • Viscous: F = −c vP, the second law of Moffatt and Shimomura.

    Verification checks

    Every run reports whether contact is maintained (the gap rate stays at zero), whether the energy only goes down, whether N stays positive so the body never leaves the table, and whether the denominator of the N formula stays positive.

    Reference

    V. Itauriya and D. Mukherjee, Slipping-contact friction in rattleback dynamics: extension of a rolling formulation using the Moffatt–Shimomura contact laws, Department of Mechanical Engineering, IIT Kanpur. Body parameters after Kane and Levinson (1982).
